# Guidelines for the Assignment (Draft): 

## The Assignment

The Spatial Data Assignment is an assignment in one step. It builds upon the work we have done in class since 6 October. You will take a portion of either the _Zanzibar Gazette_ or Lorimer's _Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf, Oman and Central Arabia_, and "wrangle" a dataset from one of them by using the LLM(s) of your choice. 

- **Format**: Individual or pairs (maximum 2 people)
- **Length**: Approximately 1500 words (about an 8-minute read), plus visuals (screenshots or saved images from the notebook) & a dataset published to your assets folder in Github.
- **Due Date**: Monday, 30 October 2026. 
- **Medium**: a post in Github pages, with screenshots and an interactive map 

If you work in pairs, it can be written together, but should be posted on each of the team member's course site. 

We will work with the pdfs of either the _Zanzibar Gazette_ or Lorimer's _Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf, Oman and Central Arabia_  (in Drive) on a project to map some aspect of the historical Gulf region or Zanzibar that appears in them. You will use an LLM to generate data from the OCR'd digital copies we possess. Although we will not combine all of our collective datasets together, imagine that the datasets that you generate could be assembled by the instructor to create a "thick mapping" of a region of the Gulf region or Zanzibar. 

**Main Elements**

This assignment has seven main elements: (1) choosing which text you would like to work on, (2) choosing a portion of the source material and identifying the spatial elements of this humanities source material (3) modeling a spatial dataset based on those elements as they are represented in the sources, (4) prompting the LLM to extract and organize the data according to the desired structured, assessing the output and perhaps re-prompting (5) correcting the data manually, enriching with any other relevant metadata, especially the spatial data (6) visualizing the data on a map, and (7) discussion of both process and product using an article on the humanities and genAI.

**Step 1**: Choose the topic that you would like to work on from within the pdfs of either source, the Zanzibar Gazette or Lorimer's Gazetteer. You will only use a portion of either. Do not use both sources. We explored in class the sources to understand some of the information they contain--you will have to do more exploration on your own. 

You should aim to create a dataset which has about 300 data points total. There is a folder in Drive of what other students have done in the past for Zanzibar. In GitHub there are other [datasets](https://github.com/opengulf/Lorimer_data) that were made by the OpenGulf research team. You can choose to build on any of these expanding them in time or space, or you can choose an entirely new idea. 

Important to know about Lorimer is that you will work with vol 2 of it, the Statistical and Geographical section and there are 800+ chapters describing many different tribes and places in the Gulf and Arabia. All of them contain geographic information. General areas covered here include Najd (Saudi), Eastern Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, the Persian coast, Qatar, the Trucial States, and Oman. A pdf of vol 2 can be found [here](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.206964) and a digital edition [here](https://referenceworks.brill.com/display/db/loro).

Important to know about the Zanzibar Gazette is that there is much more data that is collected over time. It was, after all, an official governmental newspaper, but only a smaller amount of which has concentrated geographic information. The Zanzibar Gazette can be found in Drive for download. 

Here are some ideas to choose from: 

- Imports into or Exports From Zanzibar over several months or several years
- Shipping routes 
- kind of licenses issued in Zanzibar (for commercial purposes) over several months or several years
- Produce imported from Africa in the ZG
- the specific individuals, companies or merchants having "enemy ties," thereby disqualifying them from trade with Zanzibar - over the years  
- the number of fighters per tribe in Lorimer (either several tribes for few regions or a single tribe across many regions)
- the number of houses per tribe in Lorimer (either several tribes for few regions or a single tribe across many regions)
- kinds of boats (Mashuwahs, Ghunchahs, Baqarahs, Baghlahs, Shashahs [date stick boats], pearling boats, fishing boats, steamers) found in different regions in Lorimer 
- the depth of wells and quality of water (bad, sweet, indifferent quality, brackish) in Lorimer
- the width of river beds in Lorimer 
- routes from place X to place Y and the wells along the way in Lorimer 
- crops map (barley, tobacco, pomegranates, mangoes, maize, etc) for a few regions in Lorimer (either several crops for few regions or a single crop across many regions)
- sects and religious groups in Lorimer (Shi’ah, Sunni, Jews, Sabians, Christians, Hindu)
- animal map for a few regions in Lorimer (either several crops for few regions or a single crop across many regions)
- tribes map for a few regions in Lorimer
- other 

**Step 2**: There is one main difference between the Zanzibar Gazette and Lorimer's *Gazetteer*. Lorimer was compiled over a few year period, but is published largely without reference to time. The Zanzibar Gazette, as a newspaper, spans many years. If you choose Lorimer, you will need to look over multiple sections of the Gazetteer that contain similar information to capture geographic space. If you choose Zanzibar Gazette, you may need to screenshot multiple pages from the Gazette, this might mean choosing information in a similar format longitudinally in the Gazette (that is, across years). 

**Step 3**: Modeling the data. You will need to choose what data you are going to extract from the source and imagine a structured data table into which it is set. It could be helpful to review some of the principles from an article we read earlier this term: [Data Organization in Spreadsheets](https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.2017.1375989).

**Step 4**: you will semi-automate the process of data collection by using the screenshots of the pdf. Do not upload the entire pdf to an LLM. Instead extract individual pages, or use screenshots of portions of pages containing the information as we did in class. Having uploading the relevant pages, prompt the LLM to extract the data for you as we did in class on 15 October. This portion of the exercise may require some trial and error. For Step 4, you can use any tools that you feel comfortable with: chatGPT, Gemini, Mistral, Claude, etc. None of the available tools will be perfect for historical materials. You can even compare the performance of the different AI tools and will give insight into the iterative way that you went about creating the data collaboratively. You could also use the same company but different versions.   

One of my former students, R.K., who works for an AI company in Canada said to me "I find the new models are the best and then they tend to deteriorate or hallucinate more often as they are trained on new noisy data or the engineers stop paying attention to them while they focus on newer versions." Feel free to comment on this point if it is relevant. In other words, you can use the age of model as a point of comparison. 

Part of the exercise has to do with how you prompt and refine your prompts. Keep track of what you prompted and include them in your discussion and analysis below. Please provide the best prompts you use to extract the data. A callout (with the > sign in markdown) or code block would be an excellent way to do this. 

There will be some cleaning that is required to bring your dataset into shape so that it can be visualized. Please comment on what was required. 

**Step 5**: There are many ways that you can arrive at a final dataset. In particular, it may be interesting to combine some of the elements with bespoke tags (e.g. if you are working with lists of export items, you may add on a column to the data that combines all foodstuffs together, all animal products together, or all minerals together). This possible enriching of the data will be different according to the source you have chosen. Specifically for the ZG, that would mean adding a column for year or time. Optional: You could also use the technique we explored in Overpass Turbo to add a data layer from OSM that is indirectly related to the locations of your data (e.g. historical monuments in the region, farms, waterways). 

Finally, you have two options for geocoding: you can use the draft version of the Gazetteer created by the OpenGulf group for [Lorimer](https://zenodo.org/records/20122587) or by attempting to automating that process using a plugin for Google Workplace like InstaMaps or Google Apps Script so that you have latitude and longitude columns for the _Zanzibar Gazette_. Alternatively you can try the code in the notebook "Spatial Data F26."

**Step 6**:  Visualizing the data.  

You can upload your CSV to the Notebook. 

When you have created an self-contained HTML file create a folder in your `assets` folder called `maps` and push the html file to it.  Instructions for embedding the map can be found [here](https://daahnyuad.github.io/going-further-static-siteF26/#inserting-an-interactive-map-using-an-iframe).

**Step 7**:

The last step is reflecting on the entire process, what you were able to create from Lorimer or the Zanzibar Gazette and the challenges of the process.
