# Guidelines for the Assignment (Draft): 

## The Assignment

The Spatial Data Assignment is an assignment in one step. It builds upon the work we have done in class since 6 October. You will take a portion of either the _Zanzibar Gazette_ or Lorimer's _Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf, Oman and Central Arabia_, and "wrangle" a dataset from one of them by using the LLM(s) of your choice. 

- **Format**: Individual or pairs (maximum 2 people)
- **Length**: Approximately 1500 words (about an 8-minute read), plus visuals (screenshots or saved images from the notebook) & a dataset published to your assets folder in Github.
- **Due Date**: Monday, 30 October 2026. 
- **Medium**: a post in Github pages, with screenshots and an interactive map 

If you work in pairs, it can be written together, but should be posted on each of the team member's course site. 

We will work with the pdfs of either the _Zanzibar Gazette_ or Lorimer's _Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf, Oman and Central Arabia_  (in Drive) on a project to map some aspect of the historical Gulf region or Zanzibar that appears in them. You will use an LLM to generate data from the OCR'd digital copies we possess. Although we will not combine all of our collective datasets together, imagine that the datasets that you generate could be assembled by the instructor to create a "thick mapping" of a Gulf region or Zanzibar. 

This assignment has seven main elements: (1) choosing which text you would like to work on, (2) choosing a portion of the source material and identifying the spatial elements of this humanities source material (3) modeling a spatial dataset based on those elements as they are represented in the sources, (4) prompting the LLM to extract and organize the data according to the desired structured, assessing the output and perhaps re-prompting (5) correcting the data manually, enriching with any other relevant metadata, especially the spatial data (6) visualizing the data on a map, and (7) discussion of both process and product using an article on the humanities and genAI.

**Step 1**: Choice of topic that you would like to work on from within the pdfs of either source. Do not use both sources. You should aim to create a dataset which has about 300 rows of data total. **Please check with the instructor if you use one which is not included below**. There is a folder in Drive of what other students have done in the past for Zanzibar. In GitHub there are other [datasets](https://github.com/opengulf/Lorimer_data) that were made by the OpenGulf research team. You can choose to build on these expanding them in time or space, or you can choose an entirely new idea. 

- Imports/Exports into Zanzibar over the years
- Shipping routes 
- Licenses Issued (for commercial purposes)
- Produce imported from Africa 
- crops map for a few regions in Lorimer
- tribes map for a few regions in Lorimer
- other 

**Step 2**: There is one main difference between the Zanzibar Gazette and Lorimer. Lorimer was compiled in a few year period and does not extend over time. Zanzibar spans many years. If you choose Lorimer, you will need to look over geographic space for sections of the Gazetteer that contain similar information. 

If you choose the Gazette, you may need to choose multiple pages from the Gazette, this might mean choosing information in a similar format longitudinally in the Gazette (that is, across years). Your choice of topic may also need to be made based on some research about that topic across the newspaper. Remember that after 1913, the Gazette is more formalized. 

**Step 3**: Modeling the data. You will need to choose what data you are going to extract from the source and imagine a structured data table into which it is set. It could be helpful to review some of the principles from an article we read earlier this term: [Data Organization in Spreadsheets](https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.2017.1375989).

**Step 4**: you will semi-automate the process of data collection by using the pdf, or the text layer of the pdf or both. Do not upload the entire file to an LLM. Instead extract individual pages, or use screenshots of portions of pages containing the information. Having uploading the relevant pages, prompt it to extract the data for you. This portion of the exercise may be trial and error. For this, you can use any tools that you feel comfortable with: chatGPT, Gemini, Mistral, Claude, etc. None of the available tools will be perfect a sufficient job with historical materials.  You can even compare the performance of the different AI tools and will give insight into the iterative way that you went about creating the data collaboratively. You could also use the same company but different versions.   

One of my former students, R.K., who works for an AI company in Canada said to me "I find the new models are the best and then they tend to deteriorate or hallucinate more often as they are trained on new noisy data or the engineers stop paying attention to them while they focus on newer versions." Feel free to comment on this point if it is relevant. In other words, you can use the age of model as a point of comparison. 

Part of the exercise is about how you prompt and refine your prompts. Keep track of what you prompted and consider that part of your analysis below. Please provide the best prompts you use to extract the data. A callout (with the > sign in markdown) would be an excellent way to do this. 

There will be some cleaning that is required to bring your dataset into shape so that it can be visualized. Please comment on what was required. 

**Step 5**: There are many ways that you can arrive at a final dataset. In particular, it may be interesting to combine some of the elements with bespoke tags (e.g. if you are working with lists of export items, you may add on a column to the data that combines all foodstuffs together, all animal products together, or all minerals together). This possible enriching of the data will be different according to the source you have chosen. You could also use the technique we explored in Overpass Turbo to add a data layer from OSM that is indirectly related to the locations of your data (e.g. historical monuments in the region). 

This might be the case if you have data over time. You can bring together different tables, by the addition of a date column. 

Finally, you have two options for geocoding: you can use the draft version of the Gazetteer created by the OpenGulf group for [Lorimer](https://zenodo.org/records/20122587) or by attempting to automating that process using Geocode by Awesome Table in Google Sheets so that you have latitude and longitude columns for the _Zanzibar Gazette_. 

**Step 6**:  Visualizing the data.  

You can upload your CSV to the Notebook. 

When you have created an self-contained HTML file create a folder in your `assets` folder called `maps` and push the html file to it.  Instructions for embedding the map can be found [here](https://daahnyuad.github.io/going-further-static-siteF26/#inserting-an-interactive-map-using-an-iframe).

**Step 7**:

The last step is reflecting on the entire process, what you were able to create from Lorimer or the Zanzibar Gazette and the challenges of the process. 

# Guiding questions for this assignment:

- What kind of source is Lorimer or the ZG? 
- What kinds of data does that source provide? 
- Was the data directly or indirectly communicated? In other words, did you have to assume anything to create new columns? 
- How did you create prompt for ChatGPT, Gemini, Mistral or other tool to automate thse extraction of the information? What kind of "engineering" did you have to do to get better results with the prompts?
- How good was the automation? What did you have to change in order to clean the data? 
- what issues mentioned in the "Data Organization in Sheets" article did you encounter? 
- When you mapped the data, did you see any interesting patterns? Were there areas of concentration of points? Did mapping lead to any visible clusters?
- If you were to do this over many more pages then you did for this assignment (say, look at exports over the years or over all 800+ entries in Lorimer), what kind of results do you think the different scale would give?
- If you were to do this assignment with a source not included here, what would your ideal source be?
